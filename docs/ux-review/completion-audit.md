## Cleaner photo durability and recovery

The queue now resolves writes on transaction completion and rejects an abort even after request success. Queue updates and removal compare capture revisions in the same read/write transaction, so settling an older upload cannot remove or overwrite a retake. Legacy records without revisions remain supported. Upload callers share a pass, receive visit-specific counts and use Web Locks to serialize passes across tabs where supported. The queue removes a photo only after the recording endpoint explicitly confirms `recorded: true`; an HTTP 200 or sign-in page alone is insufficient. Manual retry preserves attempt history, applies only to the selected visit and retains bytes on failures. Storage and recording 401s offer sign-in recovery.

Browser review used synthetic photos in an isolated local review screen with no provider credentials. The saved photo survived reload and failed retries. A second visit showed no queued photos from the first. At 390px there was no horizontal overflow and all capture/retry targets were at least 44px high. Synthetic photos and the temporary review route were removed after verification. The screenshot is retained in the private workspace. Automated IndexedDB regressions cover abort after request success, committed bytes, old-upload/retake settlement, legacy queues, rejected and unconfirmed server responses, concurrent callers, scoped retries and recovery after a failed pass.

This does not prove physical-phone offline recovery, storage permission/timeout behavior or successful upload in staging. Uploading resumes while the visit is open; the UI no longer promises that a closed browser will continue uploading.

## Client-confirmed cancellation and skips

The user specified a $60 fee only for appointment-day cancellations and door turnaways. Cancellations on the previous calendar day are free even with less than 24 hours of notice. The appointment day is resolved in America/Chicago, not the browser or process timezone. The user also authorized clients to confirm changes to individual visits and the recurring schedule; rescheduling and recurring-plan editing remain implementation work, not office-approval questions.

Clients review a server-issued, five-minute cancellation quote before explicitly confirming its exact fee. Confirmation uses trusted roles/ownership, locks the recurring plan before the job, compares the appointment/plan/occurrence snapshot and current fee, and returns a recorded receipt. Retrying the same confirmation does not duplicate a fee invoice. Expired, moved, started, closed, transferred or changed-fee visits require a new review. Only the office can record a door turnaway. Unscheduled visits have no appointment-day fee; the door-turnaway fee remains explicit.

Cancellation retains the visit and assignment history, withdraws live offers even after a cleaner becomes inactive, withdraws pending preference requests and records a recurring skip so generation cannot resurrect the occurrence. Other recurring visits are unchanged. Database guards prevent stale dispatch from reopening a confirmed cancellation, old offer acceptance and new collection against the canceled clean. Unpaid service invoices are voided; the $60 fee is a distinct invoice and uses existing payment authorization/collection locking. Checkout identifies it as a cancellation fee and rejects a cleaner tip.

Captured or uncertain payments cancel the appointment immediately but pause additional collection and enter the office review queue. No second fee invoice is created until all existing payments are fully refunded and pending attempts/refunds are resolved. Office recheck uses provider reads and the existing payment ledger, then a role-checked, idempotent RPC records the fee once. It does not send a refund or charge a card.
